# Razorpay Billing Research: Subscriptions, Prepaid Wallet Top-ups, Card Auto-Recharge (India)

**Retrieval date:** 2026-09-26
**Scope:** Facts needed to build India-based SaaS billing on Razorpay: (a) plan subscriptions, (b) a prepaid service-credit wallet funded by manual card top-ups through hosted/standard checkout, (c) optional customer-authorised automatic threshold-based recharge (merchant-initiated, off-session card charges).

## Method statement

- **Blocked sources.** Direct page fetches (WebFetch) to `razorpay.com`, `www.rbi.org.in`, `m.rbi.org.in`, `website.rbi.org.in`, `rbidocs.rbi.org.in`, `kpmg.com` and `scconline.com` were refused by the network egress proxy (`EGRESS_BLOCKED`). **No RBI primary text could be opened.**
- **Web search.** WebSearch was used with `allowed_domains` `razorpay.com` and `rbi.org.in` (plus a few unrestricted queries on the 2026 RBI framework) until the session's shared web-search budget was used up. A search result is only a title, a URL and a summary written by a model. Rows that rely on one are marked OS, RBI-S or SEC, and their wording is not treated as a quote.
- **GitHub and npm (reachable). Most Razorpay evidence comes from these:**
  1. **`razorpay/markdown-docs`**: Razorpay's official Markdown export of razorpay.com/docs. Manifest `generated: 2026-04-09T12:43:04Z`, HEAD `ab3bda5`, 2,214 pages. The repo's `_url-md-map.json` maps each razorpay.com/docs URL to a file. Where a mapping exists, the Source URL column gives the canonical `https://razorpay.com/docs/...` URL. Otherwise it gives the raw GitHub URL. **The snapshot is about 5.5 months old, so the live docs may have changed.**
  2. **Official Node SDK `razorpay@2.9.8`**: npm tarball, published 2026-07. GitHub `razorpay/razorpay-node` HEAD `b9e8527`, 2026-07-21. I used its source code, its TypeScript definitions (some JSDoc comments are out of date compared with the docs) and the bundled `documents/*.md`.
- Nothing here was tested against a live or test Razorpay account.
- Amounts are in paise (INR subunits) unless stated otherwise.
- RBI, GST and legal items are for information only. They need professional (legal, compliance, tax) review.

## Unconfirmed / conflicting

1. **Debit latency.** For card recurring, Razorpay's own docs give three different timings:
   - 36 h 5 m after the pre-debit notification (API doc, R-040);
   - 36 h, with 36-72 h for `created` to become `authorized` (FAQ, R-042);
   - "raise the debit request 24 hours in advance" with a 24 h TAT (older announcements and FAQs).

   Subscriptions docs also say both 24 h and 36 h (R-073). Treat 36 h 5 m as the minimum and allow up to about 72 h or more.
2. **`max_amount` ceiling.** The card-mandate API allows at most `1500000` (₹15,000), but the registration link allows `100000000` (₹10,00,000) with AFA (R-032).
3. **Authorisation amount.** The Standard card authorisation doc says the amount "should be `100`", while the custom-integration doc says "minimum `100`" (R-035). So it is unknown whether the first top-up can go through as the mandate authorisation.
4. **Refund of the ₹1 authorisation.** Subscriptions docs say it is auto-refunded. The standalone Recurring Payments docs say the token is created after the auth payment is captured, and do not say whether it is refunded (R-075).
5. **`expire_at` default.** The docs say the card's expiry year; the SDK says 10 years (R-033).
6. **Frequencies and plan periods.** The docs list `weekly`, `monthly`, `yearly` and `as_presented`, but the SDK lists only `as_presented` and `monthly` (R-034). Plan periods in the docs include `quarterly`, which the SDK type lacks (R-063).
7. **Subscription `paused` state.** It is missing from the API/SDK status enum but present on the states page and in the webhooks (R-066). The `subscription.resumed` description says the subscription moves "to the `resumed` state", which is not a documented state.
8. **Card token pause.** The FAQ says cardholders can pause card tokens and merchants are notified by webhook, but the webhook table lists `token.paused` for UPI only (R-045, R-025).
9. **Capture timeouts.** The Dashboard allows at most 3 days, but the Orders API `manual_expiry_period` allows up to 7200 minutes (5 days) (R-010, R-011).
10. **`receipt` enforcement.** The docs say "has to be unique", but nothing documents rejection of duplicates or idempotent behaviour. A search summary claimed otherwise without any official text behind it (R-087).
11. **`payment.failed` on first-payment authorisation failure.** The payments webhook page says it is not triggered; the recurring webhook page lists it for failed authorisations (R-026).
12. **Recurring payment response.** The sample shows only `razorpay_payment_id`, while the text and SDK also mention `razorpay_order_id` and `razorpay_signature` (R-039).
13. **Debit card coverage.** The FAQ names four debit-card banks, while the supported-banks page lists more; both may be out of date (R-047).
14. **RBI circular numbers and dates** come only from search summaries or Razorpay-embedded PDF links (R-051 to R-060). Two points stand out:
    - Razorpay's announcement says "the maximum limit for registering mandates is ₹15,000" but links the December 2020 circular (which set ₹5,000). The ₹15,000 limit is attributed to June 2022.
    - The CoF deadline is stated as "starting from September 30 2022" (Razorpay) versus the commonly cited 1 Oct 2022.
15. **Digital Payments – E-mandate Framework, 2026 (R-056, R-057).** Its reference number, date, effective date and content come only from search snippets and secondary summaries. Whether it changes anything about how Razorpay implements card mandates (validity-period capture, variable-amount mandates, notification contents, opt-out) is unknown. The Razorpay docs snapshot (2026-04-09) predates it.
16. **Authentication Directions, 2025 (RBI/2025-26/79)**, in force from 1 Apr 2026: its effect on merchant-initiated recurring card debits is unknown (R-058).
17. **Disputes.** Whether funds are deducted when a dispute opens is undocumented; the docs describe deduction on `lost` (R-081).
18. **Activation.** It is unconfirmed whether the account needs Razorpay to enable Recurring Payments (confirmed as on-demand), card recurring specifically, International card recurring, RuPay SI, and saved cards/TokenHQ (5-7 working days). The pricing of tokenisation and recurring is not documented (R-028, R-049, R-093).
19. **Unscheduled charging.** It is unconfirmed whether `as_presented` card mandates allow unscheduled, variable, threshold-triggered debits for a SaaS MCC, and whether a token can have more than one pending pre-debit notification or order at a time (R-048).
20. **Test mode.** Its behaviour for standalone card Recurring Payments (pre-debit timing, token confirmation) is unconfirmed (R-102).
21. **Settlement recon days and pages (R-110).** The documentation does not say which time zone `year`/`month`/`day` use (HyFib asks for India's calendar day), in what order items are returned, or whether `skip` pages stay stable while Razorpay adds items. It also does not say when a day's report is complete after its settlements are processed.
22. **Disputes in settlement recon (R-111).** Only the `dispute_id` field is documented: which item carries a chargeback's deduction, and with what `type` and amounts, is unknown. Whether items with `settled: false` or `on_hold: true` appear in a day's report is also undocumented.
23. **Invoice periods and cycles (R-113).** HyFib counts a paid invoice for the subscription cycle that holds the middle of the invoice's billing period. Whether Razorpay's invoice periods always fall mostly inside the cycle they pay for (for example after a halt, a pause or a change of plan) is not documented.
24. **When a charge can be read.** How soon after a charge `paid_count`, the paid invoice and the payment can be fetched is not documented; HyFib re-reads a live subscription hourly once its cycle has ended.
25. **Invoice id on subscription payments.** The payment entity documents `invoice_id`; that every subscription charge's payment carries it is inferred, not stated. A payment without one is not matched to a subscription and waits for a person.
26. **Quarterly plans on cards (R-116).** How an `as-presented` card mandate behaves for a quarterly plan (pre-debit notices, bank limits) has not been checked in test mode.
27. **`expire_by` before `start_at` (R-119).** The API documents `expire_by` as the time "till when the customer can make the authorisation payment", and its example puts it a day after `start_at`. Whether Razorpay accepts an `expire_by` earlier than a future `start_at` is not documented: HyFib sends its Checkout window, which ends well before a start at the trial's end. To be checked in test mode before release (docs/RELEASE-CHECKLIST.md); a refusal is shown to the payer as Razorpay's own error, and nothing is charged.
28. **The authentication transaction of a future-start subscription (R-119).** Its amount is not documented for Subscriptions (the FAQ says only that it is auto-refunded); HyFib states no amount.
29. **Refunds of subscription payments (R-121).** That a subscription charge can be refunded like any captured payment is inferred from the pages on refunds and on Subscriptions, not stated, and no page says whether the refund changes the subscription or its invoice. HyFib assumes neither changes and never changes the plan itself (ADR-103). To be checked in test mode before release (docs/RELEASE-CHECKLIST.md).
30. **A cancellation scheduled for the cycle's end (R-122).** No page shows how to undo one, whether the fetched subscription shows it (`has_scheduled_changes` is described for updates only), or what a second cancel at the cycle's end returns. HyFib treats the cancel as final, never sends a second one, and keeps its own record of the cancel it asked for (ADR-109). To be checked in test mode before release (docs/RELEASE-CHECKLIST.md).
31. **A subscription that starts when another's cycle ends (R-119, R-122).** The pages describe a future `start_at` for trials, not for a subscription that takes over from another. That the new subscription's first charge falls at its `start_at`, and that nothing charges twice for the cycle that starts then, is inferred. HyFib also checks every charge against the plan's period, so a charge for an earlier period never moves the plan (`stale`, ADR-109). To be checked in test mode before release (docs/RELEASE-CHECKLIST.md).

## Implications for implementing auto-recharge (what must be verified with Razorpay account managers before launch)

### A. Design constraints that follow from the evidence (our inference, INF)

- **Auto-recharge through a card mandate is asynchronous and slow.** Each merchant-initiated charge on an India-issued card goes through issuer pre-debit notification: at least 36 h 5 m before a debit can happen, and 36-72 h or more before the payment reaches `authorized` (R-040, R-042).
  - The recharge trigger must fire well before the wallet runs out, for example a "low-balance" threshold sized to cover about 3-4 days of expected usage.
  - The product must define what happens while a recharge is pending: a grace or overdraft allowance, or throttling.
  - Credit the wallet **only** when the payment is `captured` (from a webhook plus an API fetch), never when the order or recurring payment is merely created.
- **Only one auto-recharge per customer should be in flight at a time.** Retries are manual and spaced at least 36 h apart on the same order (R-041). Orders created with a `notification` object get no automatic retry (R-040). A new pre-debit for the same amount replaces a pending one on RuPay (R-047).
- **Keep the mandate `max_amount` at ₹15,000 (`1500000`) or less.** SaaS is not among the ₹1,00,000 categories (R-044, R-055, INF). Above ₹15,000, every debit needs customer AFA through a link valid for 72 h, which defeats the purpose of auto-recharge.
- **Use a separate "Recurring Payments" card mandate for top-ups, not the Subscriptions mandate.** The Subscriptions product cannot make ad-hoc charges on domestic cards (R-069, R-070, R-071, R-074). Its plan updates for domestic cards are limited to offers, so an upgrade or downgrade in practice means cancelling and creating a new subscription (R-069; confirm with Razorpay).
- **Build our own idempotency.** No documented idempotency header exists for orders or `/payments/create/recurring` (R-085), and receipt enforcement is unconfirmed (R-087).
  - Persist a local recharge-attempt record keyed by our own unique `receipt` **before** calling Razorpay.
  - On a timeout, query `GET /v1/orders?receipt=` and `GET /v1/orders/:id/payments` before retrying (R-088).
  - Refunds can use `X-Refund-Idempotency`, but only through direct HTTP, not the SDK (R-086, R-089).
- **Webhook consumer requirements:**
  - Verify `X-Razorpay-Signature` over the raw body with a constant-time comparison (R-017, R-027).
  - Deduplicate on `x-razorpay-event-id` (R-018).
  - Tolerate out-of-order and duplicate events (R-020), and treat `payment.failed` as non-terminal because of late authorisation (R-009).
  - Acknowledge within 5 s and process asynchronously (R-019).
  - Run a reconciliation poller, because webhooks are disabled after 24 h of failures (R-019).
- **Mandate lifecycle handling:**
  - Charge only `confirmed` tokens (R-036).
  - Handle `token.cancelled`, `token.rejected` and possibly `token.paused` (R-025, R-045).
  - Disable auto-recharge and notify the customer when the mandate is cancelled or the card expires (`expired_at`).
  - Let customers revoke auto-recharge in-app by calling `DELETE /v1/customers/:customer_id/tokens/:token_id` (R-037). Per RBI, customer payment information must be deleted for withdrawn mandates (R-053); scope this with legal.
- **Card data storage.** Store only Razorpay ids (`customer_id`, `token_id`, `payment_id`, `order_id`) plus the permitted display metadata (last 4 digits, issuer name, network). Never store the card number or CVV (R-060, R-061).
- **Accounting and ledger.**
  - Record gross `amount`, `fee` (including GST) and `tax` for each payment (R-014, R-083).
  - Razorpay fees are not reversed on refunds (R-079).
  - Refunds and lost disputes are debited from the merchant balance (R-079, R-081).
  - Dispute outcomes (`payment.dispute.*`) should claw back unused wallet credit per our terms (INF).

## Things we must NOT assume

1. That Razorpay offers an instant, Stripe-style "off-session" card charge for India-issued cards. Domestic card mandate debits go through a pre-debit notification cycle of 36 h 5 m or more, and final status can take 36-72 h (R-040, R-042).
2. That a card saved through Flash Checkout/TokenHQ can be charged without the customer present. Only a confirmed recurring-mandate token can be charged through `/v1/payments/create/recurring` (R-093, R-095).
3. That a Subscriptions mandate can be reused for ad-hoc top-ups, or that domestic-card subscriptions can be upgraded or downgraded in place, or charged manually (R-069, R-070, R-071, R-074).
4. That `receipt` acts as an idempotency key, or that Razorpay dedupes repeated order or recurring-payment creation (R-085, R-087).
5. That the Node SDK can send idempotency headers (it drops them), or that its signature helpers compare in constant time (R-006, R-027, R-089).
6. That webhooks arrive exactly once, in order, or while our endpoint is down for more than 24 h, since they are disabled after 24 h of failures (R-018, R-019, R-020).
7. That `payment.failed` is final. Late authorisation can turn it into `authorized` or `captured` for up to 3 days (R-009, R-020).
8. That the Checkout `handler` callback means the payment succeeded. Verify the signature, then confirm `captured` through the API or a webhook before crediting the wallet (R-005, R-007).
9. That authorised payments stay available. Uncaptured payments are auto-refunded after the capture timeout (default 3 days) (R-010).
10. That refunds reverse Razorpay fees (they do not), or that refunds are always instant (`optimum` falls back to normal), or that a normal refund is possible for payments older than 6 months (R-077, R-079).
11. That `max_amount` above ₹15,000 works without customer interaction for a SaaS business. The ₹1,00,000 limit covers only mutual funds, insurance and credit-card bill payments (R-044, R-055).
12. That a mandate token stays valid forever. Customers can pause or cancel at their bank, cards expire, tokens get rejected, and merchants must handle `token.*` events (R-025, R-036, R-045).
13. That we may store card numbers, CVVs or full card details. At most we may keep the last 4 digits and issuer name (plus network metadata according to Razorpay) (R-060, R-061).
14. That the RBI rules are static or that the Razorpay docs snapshot reflects them. The 2026 E-mandate Framework and the 2025 Authentication Directions postdate or coincide with the docs snapshot (R-056, R-058).
15. That all Indian debit cards support recurring payments (coverage is limited) or that RuPay supports debits above ₹15,000 (R-047).
16. That test-mode timings and behaviours match live mode, especially for card mandates (R-102).
17. That Razorpay-hosted invoices or receipts satisfy our GST invoicing obligations without professional review (R-084, R-105).
18. That `order.paid` or `payment.captured` means the money has been settled. Settlement is T+2 working days by default and is net of fees and GST, with deductions for refunds and disputes (R-082, R-083).
19. That dispute funds are deducted only at loss, or only at onset. The docs describe deduction on `lost`; onset behaviour is unconfirmed (R-081).
20. That the ₹1 mandate-authorisation payment will be refunded automatically for standalone Recurring Payments (R-075).
